## PASS DYSON-V6
**Customer:** Dyson V6 Pulising

**Reply** (109 words; image: Poor suction — what to check first):

> A Dyson V6 that pulses on and off is almost always short of airflow. A blocked or damp filter, or a blockage, makes the motor cut out to protect itself.
> 
> Start with the bin and filter:
> 1. Take the battery out or unplug it, then empty the bin.
> 2. Take out the filter and wash it in cold water until the water runs clear.
> 3. Let it dry fully for at least 24 hours before you put it back. Even a slightly damp filter can cause pulsing.
> 
> Was the filter clogged or damp, is it damaged, or was it already clean and dry? And did that stop the pulsing?

**Customer:** filter is blocked

**Reply** (115 words):

> A blocked filter is very likely why it's pulsing. With the airflow choked off, the motor keeps cutting out to protect itself.
> 
> 1. With the battery out, rinse the filter under cold running water and gently squeeze it out. Keep going until the water runs clear. Don't use soap or the dishwasher.
> 2. Leave it to dry fully for at least 24 hours somewhere warm, but not on a radiator or in a tumble dryer.
> 3. Refit it, charge the vacuum and try it on normal mode.
> 
> Did the filter come clean, and is it running steadily now? If it's torn or stays clogged after washing, let me know and I'll find you a replacement.

**Customer:** the filter is actually damaged

**Reply** (110 words; parts: Hepa Post Motor Filter Non Genuine, Pre Filter Assembly):

> A damaged filter can't be repaired and needs replacing. A torn or misshapen filter lets dust through and restricts airflow, which can cause the pulsing.
> 
> The V6 has two filters, so make sure you order the right one:
> 1. Pre-filter: the cone-shaped filter that lifts out of the top of the bin. It's the Pre Filter Assembly.
> 2. Post-motor filter: the round cap at the back of the motor. It's the Hepa Post Motor Filter (non-genuine), £15.00.
> 
> Fit the new filter with the battery out, then charge the vacuum and try it on normal mode.
> 
> Which filter is damaged? And once the new one is in, has the pulsing stopped?

## PASS OC-02
**Customer:** indesit oven stoped heating, fan goes round n light on but its stone cold after 20 mins. grill werks fine tho. any ideas b4 i call some1 out

**Reply** (120 words):

> Since the fan turns and the grill heats, the fan motor and power supply are working. The most likely cause is the round element behind the fan at the back, which has probably failed. The function switch, thermostat or a burnt wire to that element could also be to blame.
> 
> First, a quick check. Defrost runs the fan with no heat, so it's easy to mix up:
> 1. Make sure the function knob is on the fan-oven heating symbol, a fan with a ring around it, and not defrost or light only.
> 2. Turn the temperature knob to 180°C.
> 3. Check whether the temperature light comes on.
> 
> Was it already on that setting? And does the temperature light come on?

**Customer:** clock is set, nothing flashing. its an IFW 6330 IX. switched off at the cooker switch and took the back panel off, theres a blister and a split in the round element near the bottom with a black mark

**Reply** (109 words; parts: 1800w Fan Element):

> Good find. A blistered, split fan element with a black burn mark has burnt out, and that's why the fan turns but the oven stays cold. It needs replacing. For your IFW 6330 IX that's the 1800W fan element, £30.00, and it's usually a simple swap:
> 1. Keep it switched off at the cooker switch.
> 2. Before you fit the new element, check the two terminals and the wires on them. If any are burnt or discoloured, replace those connectors too.
> 3. Push the connectors firmly onto the new element, screw it in place and refit the back panel and fan cover.
> 
> How did the terminals and wires look?

## FAIL WM-01
**Customer:** Hi, my Bosch WAN28281GB has stopped half way through a wash and is showing E18. Water still in the drum. What does that mean?

**Reply** (125 words):

> E18 on your Bosch means the machine couldn't drain, or took too long. The most likely cause is a blocked pump filter (coin trap), so that's the first thing to check. A kinked or blocked drain hose is the next most likely.
> 
> 1. Switch off and unplug. Put towels and a shallow tray down. If you've just run a hot wash, let the water cool first.
> 2. Open the small flap at the bottom front. If there's a little drain hose behind it, pull it out, take the cap off and let the water drain into the tray.
> 3. Unscrew the filter anticlockwise slowly, clear out any fluff, coins or other debris, then screw it back in firmly.
> 
> What did you find in the filter?

**Customer:** cleaned the filter, there was a sock and a hair grip in it. ran a drain and spin and it drains fast now, E18 gone

**Reply** (120 words):

> Good work. The sock and hair grip were blocking the pump filter, which is what caused E18. A sock usually gets into the pump from the door seal or the drum, so it's worth one last check:
> 
> 1. Run a short normal wash with a light load.
> 2. When it drains and spins, check the floor under the filter flap stays dry. That tells you the filter cap is screwed in straight and fully.
> 3. Before your next wash, look round the folds of the door seal for anything else small that's trapped.
> 
> If E18 comes back, or it drains slowly again, let me know and we'll check the drain hose next. Did the test wash finish without any leaks?

## PASS DW-04

**Reply** (119 words):

> 
> Please don't run it again for now. Do this first:
> 1. Switch it off at the wall and unplug it.
> 2. Turn off the water tap that feeds the dishwasher, under the sink.
> 3. Check the inlet hose and drain hose under the sink for drips, a loose fitting or a split.
> 
> Did you find anything wet or loose, or was everything dry?

## PASS HB-02
**Customer:** Just had a new induction hob fitted, Beko. When I put my saucepan on it just flashes a U and doesn't heat. Is it faulty? It's brand new!!

**Reply** (121 words):

> Good news: it's very unlikely to be faulty. On a Beko induction hob, a flashing U means it can't detect a suitable pan. That's normal behaviour when the pan isn't magnetic or isn't big enough for the zone. Aluminium, copper and some stainless pans won't work on induction.
> 
> 1. Hold a fridge magnet against the bottom of your saucepan. It needs to stick firmly.
> 2. If it doesn't, the pan isn't induction-compatible. Try a cast iron or induction-marked pan instead.
> 3. Make sure the pan is flat and covers most of the ring marked on the hob.
> 
> It would only be a fault if a pan the magnet sticks to firmly still shows U on every zone. Did the magnet stick?

> This is very common after a power cut and it's almost never a fault. When the power comes back, the clock resets and goes into automatic (timed) mode, and the oven won't heat until you put it back into manual and set the time.

## PASS TD-04
**Customer:** We bought a new heat pump tumble dryer last month and a normal cotton load takes over 3 hours. Our old vented one took about an hour. Is it faulty?

**Reply** (130 words):

> That's very likely normal. Heat pump dryers dry at a lower temperature than vented ones, so they take a lot longer and clothes come out warm rather than hot. A long cotton programme is typical. It would be a fault if the clothes were still wet at the end of a full programme, even with clean filters and an empty water tank.
> 
> To get the best drying times:
> 1. Switch the dryer off and clean the fluff filter in the door opening.
> 2. Take out the second filter or condenser behind the bottom front flap, rinse it under the tap and refit it.
> 3. Use a high spin speed on your washer and fill the drum no more than two-thirds.
> 
> After a full programme, are the clothes coming out dry?

## PASS DW-05
**Customer:** My dishwasher leaves plastic tubs and the inside of the door wet at the end of the eco programme. Glasses are fine. Is it broken?

**Reply** (132 words):

> That sounds normal, not broken. Plastic holds very little heat, so water stays on it. Eco programmes also do little or no heated drying, so condensation is left on plastics and on the inside of the door. Your glasses coming out dry is a good sign. > 1. Top up the rinse aid. If it's already full, turn up the dose.

## PASS FF-11
**Customer:** freezer alarm keeps beeping, i think the door was left open a bit overnight. the display says -9

**Reply** (119 words):

> That sounds like the normal high-temperature alarm, not a fault. -9 is the freezer's current temperature (it should be around -18). Leaving the door open overnight let it warm up, so it's warning you.
> 
> 1. Check nothing is stopping the door closing, like a drawer, bag or ice build-up, and that the seal sits flat all round.
> 2. Press the alarm or alarm-off button to silence it. Many models have one on the display panel.
> 3. Keep the door shut and give it several hours to get back down to temperature.
> 
> It would be a fault if it hasn't got below about -15 within 12 to 24 hours, or if the door won't seal. Does the door shut properly?
