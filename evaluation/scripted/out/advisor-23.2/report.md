## PASS DYSON-V6
**Customer:** Dyson V6 Pulising

**Reply** (123 words; image: Poor suction — what to check first):

> A Dyson V6 that pulses on and off is almost always restricted airflow. A blocked or damp filter, or a blockage, makes the motor cut in and out to protect itself.
> 
> The best first check is the filter:
> 1. With it switched off, empty the bin.
> 2. Twist out the filter at the top of the cyclone. Tap off the dust, then rinse it in cold water until the water runs clear. Don't use soap.
> 3. Leave it to dry fully for at least 24 hours before you put it back. Even a slightly damp filter can cause pulsing.
> 
> Was the filter clogged or damp, is it damaged, or was it already clean and dry? Once it's back in, is it still pulsing?

**Customer:** filter is blocked

**Reply** (115 words):

> A blocked filter is almost certainly why it's pulsing. The motor can't get enough air, so it keeps cutting out to protect itself.
> 
> To clear it:
> 1. With it switched off, tap the loose dust out of the filter over a bin.
> 2. Rinse it under cold running water and gently squeeze it until the water runs clear. Don't use soap or put it in the dishwasher.
> 3. Leave it somewhere warm to dry fully for at least 24 hours. Don't use a hairdryer or radiator. Then refit it.
> 
> If it won't rinse clean, or it's torn or misshapen, it needs replacing.
> 
> Once it's dry and back in, is it running steadily or still pulsing?

**Customer:** the filter is actually damaged

**Reply** (126 words; parts: Pre Filter Assembly):

> 
> Until it arrives:

> 2. When the new filter comes, check that it's dry, twist it fully into place and charge the vacuum.
> 3. Try it on normal mode, not boost.
>

## PASS WM-01
**Customer:** Hi, my Bosch WAN28281GB has stopped half way through a wash and is showing E18. Water still in the drum. What does that mean?

**Reply** (124 words):

> E18 on your Bosch means the machine couldn't drain, or took too long to drain. The usual cause is a blocked pump filter (coin trap), often clogged with fluff, coins or hair grips. A kinked or blocked drain hose is the next most likely cause.
> 
> Start by cleaning the pump filter:
> 1. Switch off and unplug the machine. Put towels and a shallow tray in front of it.
> 2. Open the small flap at the bottom front. If there's a small drain hose there, let the water out through it a bit at a time.
> 3. Slowly unscrew the filter anticlockwise, clear any debris, check the impeller behind it turns freely, then screw the filter back in firmly.
> 
> What did you find in there?

**Customer:** cleaned the filter, there was a sock and a hair grip in it. ran a drain and spin and it drains fast now, E18 gone

**Reply** (114 words):

> Great work. The sock and hair grip were blocking the pump filter, which is why it couldn't drain and showed E18. Since it now drains quickly and the code has gone, the machine is fine. There's nothing to replace.
> 
> One last check, because the filter has just been opened:
> 1. Run a short wash with a small load.
> 2. Watch the floor around the filter flap during the drain and spin.
> 3. If it drips there, switch off and unplug, then screw the filter cap in straight and fully tight.
> 
> To stop it happening again, wash socks and small items in a mesh bag and check pockets for hair grips.
> 
> Did it stay dry?

## PASS HB-02

**Reply** (115 words):

> It's very unlikely your new hob is faulty. On Beko induction hobs a flashing U usually means it can't detect a suitable pan, and that's normal behaviour. >

> 2. Check the base sits flat and covers most of the zone.
> 3. If it passes both, try it on another zone.

**Reply** (134 words):

> That sounds normal, not broken. Plastic holds very little heat, so water doesn't evaporate off it the way it does off glass and china. >

> 1. Top up the rinse aid. If it's already full, turn the dose up a setting.

> 3. If that's not enough, try a normal programme instead of Eco now and then.
>

## PASS FF-11
**Customer:** freezer alarm keeps beeping, i think the door was left open a bit overnight. the display says -9

**Reply** (134 words):

> That sounds like the freezer's temperature alarm doing its job. With the door open a bit overnight it has warmed to -9°C, and it will keep beeping until it gets cold again. That's normal, not a fault.
> 
> 1. Check nothing is sticking out and stopping the door, then shut it firmly so the seal closes all the way round.
> 2. Press the alarm or temperature button to silence the beeping. Most models have one.
> 3. Keep the door shut and give it a few hours to get back down towards -18°C.
> 
> Food that is still hard-frozen is fine. Anything that has thawed shouldn't be refrozen.
> 
> It's a fault if the alarm keeps going with the door shut, or if it isn't back near -18°C within 24 hours. Did the door close and seal properly?
