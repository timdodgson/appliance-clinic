## PASS DYSON-V6
**Customer:** Dyson V6 Pulising

**Reply** (124 words; image: Poor suction — what to check first):

> A Dyson V6 that pulses on and off is almost always short of airflow. A blocked or damp filter, or a blockage, makes the motor cut out to protect itself.
> 
> The best place to start is the filter:
> 1. Switch it off and take it off the charger. Empty the bin.
> 2. Twist the filter out of the top of the machine. Rinse it in cold water until the water runs clear. Don't use soap.
> 3. Let it dry for at least 24 hours before you put it back. Even a slightly damp filter can cause pulsing.
> 
> Was the filter clogged or damp, or was it already clean and dry? If it was clean, let me know and we'll look for a blockage next.

**Customer:** filter is blocked

**Reply** (107 words):

> A blocked filter is very likely the cause. It starves the motor of air, so the motor keeps cutting out to protect itself.
> 
> 1. Keep rinsing the filter in cold water and squeeze it gently until the water runs clear. Don't use soap.
> 2. Leave it to dry fully for at least 24 hours somewhere warm, but not on a radiator or near direct heat.
> 3. Put it back once it's completely dry and try the vacuum again.
> 
> If the filter won't rinse clean or is torn or misshapen, you'll need a new one.
> 
> Once it's back in, does it run steadily, or is it still pulsing?

**Customer:** the filter is actually damaged

**Reply** (101 words; parts: Pre Filter Assembly):

> A damaged filter needs replacing. It can't filter properly or let air through evenly, so the motor will keep cutting out. The filter you twist out of the top of a V6 is the pre-motor filter. I've shown the matching Pre Filter Assembly for your V6 below.
> 
> When the new one arrives:
> 1. Switch the vacuum off and take it off the charger.
> 2. Twist the new filter into the top until it sits firmly.
> 3. Empty the bin and try the vacuum on normal mode.
> 
> Once the new filter is in, does it run steadily, or is it still pulsing?

**Reply** (128 words):

> A burning rubber smell just before the brush stopped almost always means the drive belt has slipped or snapped. That usually happens because hair or thread jammed the brushbar, and suction through the hose is fine because the motor itself is OK.
> 
> Next, check the belt and brushbar:
> 1. Switch off and unplug, then lay the vacuum face down.
> 2. Undo the screws or clips on the base plate and lift out the brushbar.
> 3. Look at the belt to see whether it's snapped, stretched or glazed, and cut away any hair wrapped round the bar and its ends.
> 
> What did you find? If the belt is damaged it needs replacing, so please send the model number too. It's usually on a label on the back or underneath.

**Customer:** turned it over, loads of hair n a tassel round the ends, cut it off and it spins free now. took the cover off and the belt is snapped in 2 bits. its a U85-AS-Be

**Reply** (100 words; parts: Non Gen 3M-207-6 Tooth Belt):

> Good work. The hair and tassel jammed the brushbar, the belt overheated (that was the burning rubber smell) and it snapped. The belt needs replacing. For your U85-AS-Be the matching part is a toothed drive belt at £7.00, shown below.
> 
> To fit it:
> 1. Keep the vacuum unplugged with the base plate off.

## PASS OC-02
**Customer:** indesit oven stoped heating, fan goes round n light on but its stone cold after 20 mins. grill werks fine tho. any ideas b4 i call some1 out

**Reply** (120 words):

> The fan turning shows the fan motor is fine, and the grill working shows power is getting to the oven. The most likely cause is the fan-oven element, the ring element behind the fan, but it isn't proven yet. A burnt connector, the function switch or the thermostat can cause the same thing.
> 
> First, rule out the setting, because defrost runs the fan with no heat:
> 1. Turn the function knob to the fan symbol with a ring around it, not the plain fan (defrost), light-only or eco.
> 2. Turn the temperature to 180°C and check whether the temperature light comes on.
> 3. Leave it for 15 minutes.
> 
> Does it heat up now? And did the temperature light come on?

**Customer:** clock is set, nothing flashing. its an IFW 6330 IX. switched off at the cooker switch and took the back panel off, theres a blister and a split in the round element near the bottom with a black mark

**Reply** (115 words; parts: 1800w Fan Element):

> That's your fault. A split, blistered element with a black burn mark has failed and needs replacing. The 1800W fan element for the IFW 6330 IX is £30.
> 
> Before you order, with the oven still switched off at the cooker switch:
> 1. Look at the two spade connectors where the element goes through the back wall.
> 2. Check them for scorching, melted plastic or loose fit.

## PASS WM-01
**Customer:** Hi, my Bosch WAN28281GB has stopped half way through a wash and is showing E18. Water still in the drum. What does that mean?

**Reply** (124 words):

> E18 on your Bosch means the machine couldn't drain in time. The usual cause is a blocked pump filter, often from coins, hair grips or fluff. Sometimes it's a kinked or blocked drain hose.
> 
> Clean the pump filter first:
> 1. Switch off and unplug. Open the small flap at the bottom front and put down towels and a shallow tray.
> 2. Drain the water slowly. Use the small emergency hose if there is one, or unscrew the filter a little at a time. It will be a lot of water.
> 3. Take the filter out, clear any debris, check the pump impeller turns freely, then screw the filter back in firmly.
> 
> What did you find in the filter, and does it drain properly now?

**Customer:** cleaned the filter, there was a sock and a hair grip in it. ran a drain and spin and it drains fast now, E18 gone

**Reply** (106 words):

> Good work. The sock and hair grip were blocking the pump filter, which is what caused the E18. Now that it drains fast and the code has gone, it's fixed. You don't need any parts.
> 
> One last check to make sure it's all sealed properly:
> 1. Make sure the filter cap is screwed in fully and straight.
> 2. Run a normal wash and check the floor near the filter flap during the drain and spin.
> 3. In future, wash small items like socks in a mesh laundry bag, and check pockets for hair grips and coins.
> 
> Does it finish the wash with the floor still dry?

## PASS HB-02
**Customer:** Just had a new induction hob fitted, Beko. When I put my saucepan on it just flashes a U and doesn't heat. Is it faulty? It's brand new!!

**Reply** (122 words):

> Good news: it's very unlikely to be faulty. On Beko induction hobs a flashing U usually means no suitable pan has been found. Induction only heats pans with a magnetic, flat base that is big enough for the zone. Aluminium, copper and some stainless steel pans won't work.
> 
> 1. Hold a fridge magnet against the bottom of your saucepan. It should stick firmly.
> 2. If it doesn't stick, or the base is small or warped, try another pan that the magnet does stick to.
> 3. Put that pan in the middle of the zone and switch the zone on.
> 
> If a pan the magnet sticks to still won't heat on any zone, that would point to a fault. Did the magnet stick?

## PASS TD-04
**Customer:** We bought a new heat pump tumble dryer last month and a normal cotton load takes over 3 hours. Our old vented one took about an hour. Is it faulty?

**Reply** (135 words):

> That's very likely normal. A heat-pump dryer works like a fridge in reverse and dries at a lower temperature, so a full cotton load often takes around 3 hours, and clothes come out warm rather than hot. It would be a fault if clothes are still wet at the end of a full cycle, or cycles keep getting longer, even with clean filters and an empty water tank.
> 
> Heat-pump dryers are sensitive to fluff, so it's worth checking the filters:
> 1. Switch the dryer off and unplug it.
> 2. Clean the fluff filter in the door opening.
> 3. Open the flap at the bottom front, take out the second filter, rinse it under the tap, let it drip-dry and refit it.
> 
> Did the filters have fluff in them, and are clothes dry at the end?

## PASS DW-05
**Customer:** My dishwasher leaves plastic tubs and the inside of the door wet at the end of the eco programme. Glasses are fine. Is it broken?

**Reply** (131 words):

> That sounds normal, not broken. Plastic holds very little heat, so water doesn't dry off it the way it does from glass, and Eco programmes dry at a lower temperature. Wetness on the inside of the door is condensation, which is how many dishwashers dry. It would be a fault if glasses and china also came out wet, or if everything was cold at the end.
> 
> The most useful thing to check is the rinse aid, which makes water run off instead of sitting in drops:
> 1. Find the rinse aid cap next to the detergent drawer.
> 2. Check the level and top it up if it's low.
> 3. When the programme ends, open the door a little and leave it for 15 to 20 minutes.
> 
> Was the rinse aid low?

**Reply** (126 words):

> That sounds like the freezer's temperature alarm working as designed. -9 is most likely the current freezer temperature, not an error code. With the door ajar overnight it has warmed up from its normal -18 or so. At -9 the food should still be frozen.
