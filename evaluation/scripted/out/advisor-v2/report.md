## FAIL DYSON-V6
**Customer:** Dyson V6 Pulising

**Reply** (101 words; image: Poor suction — what to check first):

> A Dyson V6 that pulses on and off is almost always restricted airflow. A blocked or damp filter or a blockage makes the motor cut out to protect itself.
> 
> The best first check is the filters:
> 1. Take the battery out or unplug the charger, then empty the bin.
> 2. Wash the filter in cold water only, with no soap, until the water runs clear.
> 3. Let it dry fully for at least 24 hours before refitting it. Even a slightly damp filter causes pulsing.
> 
> Was the filter clogged or damp, is it damaged, or was it already clean and dry?

**Customer:** filter is blocked

**Reply** (110 words):

> That blocked filter is very likely the whole problem. With no airflow the motor overheats and cuts in and out to protect itself.
> 
> 1. With the battery out, rinse the filter under the cold tap without soap. Keep going until the water runs clear, then tap out the excess.
> 2. Leave it somewhere warm and airy for at least 24 hours until it's completely dry. Don't use a radiator or hairdryer.
> 3. Refit it, charge the vacuum fully and try it on normal power.
> 
> Is it running steadily now, or still pulsing? If the filter won't wash clean or looks torn, let me know and I'll find you a replacement.

**Customer:** the filter is actually damaged

**Reply** (112 words; parts: Hepa Post Motor Filter Non Genuine, Pre Filter Assembly):

> A damaged filter needs replacing. A torn filter can also let dust into the motor, so it's best not to keep using it.
> 
> The V6 has two filters, so check which one is damaged:
> 1. Pre-motor filter: the long one that lifts out of the top of the cyclone. That's the Pre Filter Assembly.
> 2. Post-motor filter: the round one at the back of the motor. That's the Hepa Post Motor Filter (non-genuine).
> 
> I've shown both below with their prices. Fit the new one dry, charge the vacuum fully and try it on normal power. Which filter was damaged, and once the new one is in, does it run steadily without pulsing?

## PASS WM-01
**Customer:** Hi, my Bosch WAN28281GB has stopped half way through a wash and is showing E18. Water still in the drum. What does that mean?

**Reply** (120 words):

> E18 on your Bosch means the machine couldn't drain, or took too long to drain. The usual cause is a blocked pump filter (coin trap), often from a sock, coins or fluff. Sometimes it's a kinked or blocked drain hose.
> 
> Check the filter first:
> 1. Switch off and unplug. If you've been washing hot, let the water cool. Put towels and a shallow tray by the small flap at the bottom front.
> 2. Open the flap. Pull out the small drain tube, take the cap off and let the water run into the tray. Refill the cap as needed.
> 3. Slowly unscrew the filter anticlockwise, clear any debris, then screw it back in firmly.
> 
> What did you find in there?

**Customer:** cleaned the filter, there was a sock and a hair grip in it. ran a drain and spin and it drains fast now, E18 gone

**Reply** (112 words):

> Great work. The sock and hair grip were blocking the pump filter, and that's what caused E18. If it drains fast now and the code has gone, it's fixed.
> 
> One last check, since the filter has been opened:
> 1. Make sure the filter is screwed in fully and sitting straight. Leave the drain tube capped and clipped back in place.
> 2. Run a normal wash and check the floor at the bottom front during the drain and spin.
> 3. To stop it happening again, put socks and small items in a mesh wash bag, and empty pockets for hair grips and coins.
> 
> Did the next wash finish dry and without a code?

## PASS DW-04
**Customer:** Bosch integrated dishwasher showing E:15 and the pump keeps running. Model SMV4HTX31E. I've turned it off and on, still there

**Reply** (135 words):

> On your Bosch, E:15 means the anti-flood protection has gone off. Water has got into the tray in the base, a float has detected it, and the machine is running the drain pump on purpose to protect your kitchen. The pump isn't the problem. A leak somewhere is, and turning it off and on won't clear the code while the base is still wet.
> 
> First, stop any more water getting in and check the easiest leak points:
> 1. Switch off at the wall, unplug it and turn off the dishwasher tap under the sink.
> 2. Look at the inlet hose where it joins the tap for drips, a loose fitting or a split.
> 3. Do the same along the drain hose and where it joins the sink waste.
> 
> Did you find anything wet or loose?

## PASS TD-03
**Customer:** Bosch WTN85201GB condenser dryer not drying properly anymore, clothes still damp after 2 hrs. any ideas

**Reply** (124 words; image: Tumble dryer runs but doesn't heat — what to check):

> Clothes still damp after 2 hours in a condenser dryer usually means poor airflow. The most likely cause is a fluff filter or condenser unit clogged with fluff. That's free to fix and needs no parts.
> 
> 1. Switch the dryer off, unplug it and let it cool.
> 2. Pull out the fluff filter in the door opening. Clear all the fluff, and rinse the mesh if it looks coated.
> 3. Open the flap at the bottom front and take out the condenser unit. Rinse it under the tap until the water runs clear through the fins, let it drip-dry, then put it back in firmly.
> 
> Was either one clogged, or were they already clean? Once both are clean, does a normal load dry properly?

## PASS HB-02
**Customer:** Just had a new induction hob fitted, Beko. When I put my saucepan on it just flashes a U and doesn't heat. Is it faulty? It's brand new!!

**Reply** (121 words):

> Good news, it's very unlikely to be faulty. On Beko induction hobs a flashing U usually means "no suitable pan detected". Induction only heats pans with a magnetic base that's flat and big enough to cover the zone. Aluminium, copper and some stainless pans won't work.
> 
> Quick check:
> 1. Hold a fridge magnet against the bottom of the saucepan.
> 2. If it sticks firmly, put the pan in the middle of a zone that matches its size and switch on.
> 3. If the magnet doesn't stick, or only just holds, try another pan that it sticks to firmly.
> 
> If a pan the magnet sticks to still shows U on every zone, that would point to a fault. Does the magnet stick?

## PASS OC-08
**Customer:** oven wont come on, display is flashing 0.00, we had a power cut last night

**Reply** (129 words):

> This is very common after a power cut. When the power comes back, the clock often resets and switches to automatic (timed) mode. In that mode the oven won't heat until you set it back to manual. It's almost never a faulty part.
> 
> 1. Turn the oven controls to off.
> 2. Press the manual button (it usually has a hand symbol). On many ovens you press two buttons together instead, often the + and – buttons.
> 3. Set the correct time. The flashing should stop and a hand symbol or "A" may appear or go away.
> 
> Please don't switch it off at the wall to reset it, because that just puts it back into automatic mode.
> 
> Does it heat once it's in manual? If not, what make is it?

## PASS TD-04
**Customer:** We bought a new heat pump tumble dryer last month and a normal cotton load takes over 3 hours. Our old vented one took about an hour. Is it faulty?

**Reply** (136 words):

> That's very likely normal. A heat-pump dryer works like a fridge in reverse. It dries at a much lower temperature than a vented dryer, so around three hours for a full cotton load is common, and clothes come out warm rather than hot. It would be a fault if clothes are still wet at the end of a full cycle, or if drying times keep getting longer.
> 
> To make sure it's working at its best:
> 1. Switch it off and unplug it.
> 2. Clean the fluff filter in the door. Then rinse the second filter behind the bottom front flap under the tap and let it drip-dry.
> 3. Empty the water tank, then run a normal cotton load with the drum no more than two-thirds full.
> 
> Do the clothes come out fully dry at the end?

## PASS FF-11
**Customer:** freezer alarm keeps beeping, i think the door was left open a bit overnight. the display says -9

**Reply** (141 words):

> That sounds like the freezer's high-temperature alarm, and it's doing its job. The -9 is most likely the current temperature, not an error code. With the door open overnight it has warmed up from the usual -18°C, and it will beep until it cools back down.
> 
> 1. Check nothing is sticking out and stopping the door, then close it firmly and make sure the seal sits flat all the way round.
> 2. Press the alarm or freezer button to silence the beeping. Most models have one, and your manual will say which.
> 3. Leave the door shut and check the display over the next few hours. It should keep dropping towards -18.
> 
> If it stops going down, or the alarm keeps coming back once the door is shut, that's a fault. Did the door shut properly, and is the temperature falling?
