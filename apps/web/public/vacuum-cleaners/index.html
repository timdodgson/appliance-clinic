Vacuum cleaner help

A vacuum is a fan (the motor) pulling air through a hose, filters, and a bag or bin. Loss of suction is almost always a full bin, a choked filter, or a blockage in the wand — not a “dead motor” — until those are cleared. Cordless models add a battery that can look like a motor fault when it is just empty or tired.

Common problems

Short, honest explanations — not a parts catalogue. If it matches what you are seeing, you can take that wording into a diagnosis.

Weak suction

Work from the head back: clogged floor tool, blocked wand (a sock or Lego is a classic), full bag or bin, and filters packed with dust. A machine that sucks strongly at the hose inlet but not at the head has a blockage in the tools, not a new motor.

Diagnose this

Suction pulses or surges in waves

Pulsing — suction that throbs or revs up and down — is almost always an airflow problem, whatever the make. A near-blocked filter, a full bin, or a partial clog in the wand or head starves the motor of air, so it hunts and surges. Work the airflow path first: empty the bin, clean or dry the filters, and clear the hose and floor head. Only if the airflow is genuinely clear and a cordless model still pulses with a short runtime is the battery worth a look — pulsing on its own is not a battery verdict.

Diagnose this

Cuts out after a minute

Many motors have a thermal cut-out. A blocked machine runs hot and stops, then works again when cool. That is a blockage reminder, not a random electrical ghost. If it cuts out with a clean path, stop using it.

Diagnose this

Brush bar will not spin

Hair around the roller, a belt (on some uprights), or a clutch that disengages on a stuck bar. Cut hair out with scissors, unplugged. Do not keep running a jammed brush motor.

Diagnose this

Will not turn on (mains or cordless)

Mains: check the socket with a lamp. Cordless: charge status and a flashing battery mean power, not floorhead. A completely dead cordless after a drop can be a battery pack or a broken switch — still not something to prize open if the pack is swollen.

Diagnose this

Cordless will not charge or dies in minutes

Check the power path before the pack. Confirm the charger plug, the socket, and the charging light on the dock or tool: no light at all usually means the charger or its lead, not the battery. If it charges fully but then runs for only a couple of minutes, the pack is worn — a wear item on any make, replaced as a battery, not a sign the motor or head has failed. Do not keep charging a pack that is hot, swollen or smells.

Diagnose this

Burning smell or smoke from the motor

Unplug / isolate. A dust-packed motor or a dying armature smells of hot electrics. Do not “just finish the stairs”.

Safe checks you can make

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in Stop and get help applies.

Bin or bag, then filters

Empty the bin or replace the bag before anything clever. Wash only filters that the maker says are washable — some HEPA filters are dry-clean only and die if soaked. Dry washable filters fully before refitting or you will cook them.

Hose and wand

Drop a coin or look through the wand. Flexible hoses crush internally; a hose that looks open can still be blocked inside the corrugations.

Head and brush

Unplug. Cut hair off the roller. Check the suction inlet in the head is not taped over with a rug fringe.

Stop and get help

These are the points where another cycle, another click, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

Swollen or leaking battery (cordless)

Do not charge it. Do not put it in household rubbish. Follow local battery disposal. A puffed pack is not a “keep using until it dies” part.

Sparks from the motor bay or a trip

Stop. That is not a filter.

Used on wet (unless it is a wet vac)

A dry vacuum that has sucked water is an electrical risk. Do not switch it back on to “dry it out”.

Know your appliance

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, in plain language. This is identification, not a strip-down guide.

Motor / fan

The only moving part that makes vacuum. It fails, but far less often than blockages. Replacing it varies wildly by brand and is often uneconomic on cheap cylinder vacs.

Filters

Protect the motor. A missing filter is how motors die young.

Battery pack

On cordless, a pack that holds seconds of charge is a wear part. It is not the same as a blocked head.

Drive belt (some uprights)

A burnt, slack belt stops the brush. Visible once the sole plate is off — only if your model is meant to be opened that way.

Find your model number

Look on the underside of the head, on the motor housing, or near where the hose enters the cylinder. Cordless sticks often hide the model in the battery well or on the wand.

  • Upright and cylinder plates are easy to get dusty — wipe before the photo.
  • Battery voltage on a pack is not the vacuum model number.

When you start a diagnosis, use the camera button on the composer to photograph the plate. Conversations stay in your browser — they are not published as web pages.
