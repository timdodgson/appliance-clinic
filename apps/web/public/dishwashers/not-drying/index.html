Dishwasher not drying dishes

Here is the surprise: most modern dishwashers do not blow hot air. They dry by condensation — heat held in the load from a hot final rinse makes water evaporate and run down the cooler stainless-steel tub. So wet plastics, a few drips, and water sitting in upturned bases are often completely normal, not a broken heater. Rinse aid, the programme you pick, and whether you open the door at the end usually matter far more than any part.

Common causes

A short, ordered list of what usually explains this — most likely and most accessible first. We cannot know your exact fault from here, only point you at the right area.

Rinse aid empty or set too low

Rinse aid breaks the surface tension so water sheets off instead of beading. On a condensation dryer it is the single biggest lever on how dry things come out.

Plastics do not hold heat

Plastic cools instantly, so it cannot evaporate its own water the way glass and steel do. Plastic tubs staying wet while plates are dry is physics, not a fault.

Eco or short programme

Lower-temperature eco and quick cycles leave less heat in the load, so there is less energy to dry with. A hotter or auto programme dries better.

Door kept shut at the end

Trapped steam re-wets the load as it cools. Many machines auto-open the door for this reason; a blocked or disabled vent undoes it.

Loading and overfilling

Nested items and cups that cradle water pool rather than drain. Crockery touching also stops air and water moving.

Hard water or wrong detergent

Mineral film and residue can look like “not dried”. That is a water or detergent issue, not a drying-system failure.

A genuine heating or fan-dry fault

Only when even glass and steel stay cold and wet across hot programmes — then a heater, heat-pump or drying-fan fault is worth investigating with evidence.

Safe checks you can do

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in When to stop applies.

Top up rinse aid and turn it up

Fill the rinse-aid reservoir and nudge the dosage setting up a notch. Re-run a normal load and judge glass and steel, not plastics.

Use a hotter or auto programme

Swap the eco or quick cycle for an auto or intensive programme and compare. More heat in the final rinse means more drying.

Crack the door at the end

Open the door 10–20 minutes after the cycle to let steam escape — unless your machine auto-opens, in which case leave that vent clear.

Load so water can run off

Angle bowls and cups so they do not cradle water, keep items apart, and do not overfill the top rack with plastics.

When to stop

These are the points where another cycle, another go, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

Water near under-sink sockets

If a leak appears, isolate power — under-sink sockets and water are a bad mix. This is a leak rule, not a drying one, but worth knowing.

Burning or melted-plastic smell

A stray utensil resting on the heating element can scorch. Stop the cycle, let it cool, and remove the item.

Do not replace the heater by default

Because most “not drying” is architectural, swapping a heater or element before you have evidence usually spends money and changes nothing.

What part might be involved

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, framed as likely possibilities — not a certainty, and not a shopping list to buy before the fault is confirmed.

Rinse-aid dispenser

The cheapest, most effective “drying part” there is. Empty or faulty, it leaves everything wetter.

Heating element or heat pump

Hot water in the final rinse does the drying work indirectly. Measuring the element is an engineer test, not a customer one.

Drying fan or auto-open vent (model-dependent)

Some machines add active drying. If yours has it and it has stopped, that is a specific, evidence-led fault — not the default cause.

Find your model number

Open the door and look on the side or top edge of the door, or on the tub wall. The product or FD number tells us whether your machine uses condensation, a fan, or heat-pump drying.

  • Note whether your model is advertised with “active” or “fan” drying — many are condensation-only by design.
  • Photograph the door-edge sticker before any kitchen plinth goes back on.
