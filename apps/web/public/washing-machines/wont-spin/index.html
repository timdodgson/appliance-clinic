Washing machine won’t spin

A spin needs three things to be true: the water has drained, the door is locked, and the drum can safely accelerate. The most common reason a machine “won’t spin” is that it did not drain, or the load is unbalanced and the machine deliberately refused a high-speed spin to avoid walking across the floor. A belt or motor fault is possible, but it belongs further down the list — not as the first assumption.

Common causes

A short, ordered list of what usually explains this — most likely and most accessible first. We cannot know your exact fault from here, only point you at the right area.

Water still in the drum

If it did not drain, it will not spin. Clothes left soaking in standing water is a drain problem first — clear that before suspecting the spin side.

Unbalanced or very small load

A single towel or a bundle flung to one side makes the drum wobble. Many machines will tumble, pause, retry, and settle for a slow spin to stay safe.

Programme or option chosen

Rinse-hold, a no-spin setting, delicates or a low spin speed can all look like a spin failure. Check what was actually selected.

Door lock (interlock)

The machine will not spin until it is sure the door is shut and locked. A worn or faulty interlock can block the spin even though the door looks closed.

Drive belt (belt-driven models)

A slack or snapped belt lets the motor run while the drum barely turns. On these models the drum often spins very freely and quietly by hand.

Motor, carbon brushes or control

Last, and with evidence: a burning smell, no spin on any programme, or a motor that races without turning the drum. These are engineer findings, not a first guess.

Safe checks you can do

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in When to stop applies.

Did it drain?

Look for standing water in the drum. If there is water, treat it as a drain problem first — it will not spin until the tub is empty.

Rebalance and retry

Open up, redistribute the washing (add a towel to a single heavy item), close the door firmly and run a spin-only on a balanced load.

Check the programme and door

Make sure you are not on rinse-hold or a no-spin option, and that the door clicked shut. A door that does not latch stops the spin by design.

Belt symptom (observation only)

With the power off, a belt-driven drum that spins freely, silently and for a long time by hand can hint at a slipping or broken belt. This is an observation to describe — not a reason to open the cabinet yourself.

When to stop

These are the points where another cycle, another go, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

No live electrical testing

Testing a motor, control board or interlock with the machine powered is an engineer job. Do not probe wiring.

Burning rubber or electrical smell

Stop and isolate. A hot-belt or hot-motor smell is not something to spin through “one more time”.

Do not force the drum

Never hold or force the drum while the machine is powered and trying to spin.

What part might be involved

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, framed as likely possibilities — not a certainty, and not a shopping list to buy before the fault is confirmed.

Door interlock

A small switch in the door frame that confirms the door is shut. A common, undramatic reason a spin is blocked.

Drive belt

On belt-driven machines, the belt links motor to drum. Slipping or snapped belts are a realistic possibility worth naming.

Motor, brushes and control

Framed as a possibility only. These need an engineer and evidence, not a parts-first guess.

Find the exact fault

Find your model number

The model number is on the door rim, inside the frame, or on a rear plate. Spin problems vary a lot between direct-drive and belt-driven machines, so the exact model matters.

  • Note whether it is a direct-drive or belt-driven machine if the paperwork says.
  • Photograph the whole sticker, including the serial line.

When you start a diagnosis, use the camera button on the composer to photograph the plate. Conversations stay in your browser — they are not published as web pages.
