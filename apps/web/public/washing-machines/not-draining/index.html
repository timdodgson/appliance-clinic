Washing machine not draining

Water left in the drum means the machine cannot push it out along its drain path: drum, pump filter, drain pump, drain hose, then the standpipe or sink waste. Most no-drain faults are a blocked filter or a blocked household waste pipe rather than a dead pump — so start at the easy, accessible end before anyone talks about replacing parts. A machine that cannot drain usually will not spin either, because it is designed not to spin on a full tub.

Common causes

A short, ordered list of what usually explains this — most likely and most accessible first. We cannot know your exact fault from here, only point you at the right area.

Blocked pump filter

Coins, buttons, hair and lint collect in the filter behind the small flap at the bottom front. This is the single most common cause and the one you can often clear yourself.

Kinked or blocked drain hose

The hose behind the machine can be crushed against the wall or pushed too far down the standpipe. A machine shoved back after cleaning is a classic trigger.

Blocked sink waste or standpipe

If the machine pumps out but dirty water rises back into the drum or the sink, the blockage is often in the household waste, not the machine.

Object jamming the pump

A coin, bra wire or hair grip past the filter can stop the impeller turning. You may hear the pump try and give up.

Drain pump failure

Only after the above are cleared. A pump that is silent, or hums but moves no water, may have failed — but that is an evidence-based conclusion, not the first guess.

Safe checks you can do

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in When to stop applies.

Switch off and prepare for water

Turn the machine off at the wall before you open anything. Have a shallow tray and towels ready — standing water will come out when you open the filter flap.

Pump filter (if your model has an accessible flap)

Open the bottom-front flap, ease out any drain hose on a cap first, then twist the filter out and pick off coins, hair and lint. Refit it firmly. Typical layout — yours may differ.

Drain hose and standpipe

Look behind the machine for a kinked or crushed hose. Lift the hose out of the standpipe and check water flows away freely — if it backs up, the blockage is the house waste.

Listen on a drain or spin

Start a drain or spin and listen: a pump that hums but shifts nothing points at a jam or a failing pump; total silence points at power, the lid of the filter, or the control. Do not poke anything into the pump while it is powered.

When to stop

These are the points where another cycle, another go, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

Water near the socket or plug

Isolate the machine from a dry switch or the consumer unit. Do not reach into standing water while it is plugged in.

Burning smell or repeated trips

A pump that smells hot, or a machine that trips the breaker when it tries to drain, should stay off. Do not keep forcing another drain.

Nothing metal in the pump

Never put a screwdriver or knife into the pump housing to free the impeller while the machine has power. Isolate first and use your fingers once it is safe.

What part might be involved

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, framed as likely possibilities — not a certainty, and not a shopping list to buy before the fault is confirmed.

Pump filter

The customer-accessible strainer behind the flap. Clearing it is a check, not a repair.

Drain pump

The motor and impeller that push water out. Replacement is a repair, and only sensible once a blockage has been ruled out.

Drain hose and standpipe

The hose belongs to the machine; the standpipe and U-bend belong to the house. A blocked trap is a plumbing job, not an appliance fault.
