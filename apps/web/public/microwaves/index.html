Microwave help

A microwave cooks with high-voltage energy inside a shielded box. The door, the mesh in the glass, and the seals are part of that shield. If the motor turns the plate but food stays cold, that is a heating-chain fault — and the dangerous parts of that chain are not customer-accessible. This page will not tell you how to open the cabinet, test a magnetron, or discharge a capacitor. Those parts hold a lethal charge even after unplugging.

Common problems

Short, honest explanations — not a parts catalogue. If it matches what you are seeing, you can take that wording into a diagnosis.

Turns, lights, but food stays cold

If the lamp and turntable work, the low-voltage side is doing something. No heat then sits in the high-voltage chain (magnetron and related parts) or in a door switch that lies about the door being shut. Either way, the next step is not a screwdriver to the cover.

Diagnose this

Sparking or arcing in the cavity

Stop using it. Sparks can come from metal, gold-rimmed china, a fork left in, a damaged waveguide cover (the mica sheet on the side wall), or a failing component. Remove obvious metal. If it still arcs on a microwave-safe bowl of water, stop — the cavity is not safe to keep using.

Diagnose this

Door, latch or it will not start

Several door switches must agree the door is closed. A door that drops, a broken latch, or a microwave that runs with the door questionable is a stop-using-it fault. Never bypass a door switch.

Diagnose this

Turntable not turning

The coupler under the glass plate can split, or the motor can fail. A plate that sits still still heats — but food will cook in hot spots. Check the roller ring is the right way up before assuming a motor.

Diagnose this

Display dead, clock blank, or buttons do nothing

A blank display with no fan and no light is usually supply: try the socket with something else, and check the plug fuse. A lit display that refuses to start can be a child lock (often a hold-to-clear button) or a demo or showroom mode left on after a power cut. A keypad where only some buttons respond points at the touch panel. None of this calls for the case to come off.

Diagnose this

Cuts out, noisy, or smells of burning

A loud buzz that is new, a burning smell, or a trip at the socket: unplug. Do not run “just 30 seconds more” to finish a meal.

Safe checks you can make

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in Stop and get help applies.

A bowl of water, nothing else

Place a microwave-safe bowl of water in the centre, no metal, and run a short time. If the water does not heat at all, you have a no-heat fault. If it heats, the appliance is producing energy — look at cookware and time. Stop if you see sparks.

Cavity and waveguide cover

With it unplugged and cool, look for a burnt or missing sheet on the side wall (waveguide cover) and for food carbon. You can wipe the cavity. You cannot replace HV parts from this page.

Door mesh and seal

The metal mesh in the door is not decorative. If the glass is broken or the mesh is damaged, do not use the microwave.

Stop and get help

These are the points where another cycle, another click, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

Never remove the cover

High-voltage capacitors can hold a charge after the plug is out. There is no safe consumer test of a magnetron. If a video tells you otherwise, ignore it.

Do not use it with a damaged door

A microwave that runs with the door open, or with a broken hinge, is not a bargain to keep using.

Persistent arcing

Stop. Continued arcing damages the cavity and is a fire risk.

Know your appliance

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, in plain language. This is identification, not a strip-down guide.

Magnetron (engineer only)

Produces the microwaves. We may name it in a diagnosis. We will not ask you to access it.

Door switches

Several cheap switches in the latch assembly. They are a common “dead microwave” cause and still require competent refitting so the door cannot lie.

Waveguide cover

A small disposable sheet that keeps food out of the waveguide. A burnt one is visible without opening the outer case, through the cavity.

Turntable motor and coupler

The plastic coupler under the glass plate, and the small motor below it, are the one mechanical area that is safe to describe. A cracked coupler is a cheap, accessible part on many models and does not involve the high-voltage side.

Find your model number

Look just inside the door opening or on a rear plate. You want the model, not only the wattage printed on the front.

  • Wattage on the fascia is not a model number.
  • If you have a built-in microwave, the plate may be on the frame once the door is open.

When you start a diagnosis, use the camera button on the composer to photograph the plate. Conversations stay in your browser — they are not published as web pages.
