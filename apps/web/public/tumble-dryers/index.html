Tumble dryer help

A tumble dryer tumbles clothes through warm air, then gets rid of the moisture — either by venting it outside or by condensing it into a tank. Almost every “not drying” complaint starts with airflow. Heaters fail too, but a clogged fluff filter or condenser is the check that actually belongs to you.

Common problems

Short, honest explanations — not a parts catalogue. If it matches what you are seeing, you can take that wording into a diagnosis.

Runs but clothes stay wet

If the drum turns, the motor is doing something. Wet clothes then usually mean no useful heat or no useful airflow. Clean the filter, condenser (if it has one) and vents before assuming a heater or thermostat has failed.

Diagnose this

Drum turns but there is no warmth

Reach in mid-cycle, carefully: if the drum spins but the air is cold, the heat side has stopped while the motor runs on. On vented and older condenser models that is often a thermal cut-out tripped by a blocked filter or vent — clear the airflow first, because a new heater fitted behind a blocked filter just trips again. Heat-pump models dry cool by design, so “not very warm” is normal for them; judge those on dryness and time, not on heat.

Diagnose this

Takes too long / poor drying

Restricted airflow makes the machine work longer and run hotter. Overloading has the same effect. Heat-pump dryers especially hate a dirty filter — they dry cool and slowly when neglected, then error.

Diagnose this

Tank full / condenser wet mess

Condenser and heat-pump models collect water. If the tank is full every twenty minutes, or water appears at the bottom door, the pump-out path or condenser is blocked — or the tank is not seated.

Diagnose this

Stops partway through the cycle

A dryer that pauses or ends early is not always broken. A full water tank halts condenser and heat-pump models until you empty it; a door not quite latched, an anti-crease hold at the end of the programme, or a moisture sensor reading the load as dry can all look like a mid-cycle stop. If it stops and the cabinet is hot, treat it as overheating (below) rather than pressing start again.

Diagnose this

Too hot, scorching or cutting out

Overheating is a stop-using-it moment if you smell scorching, see browning on clothes, or the machine trips. That is airflow or a safety thermostat doing its last job. Let it cool. Do not bypass thermal cut-outs.

Diagnose this

Noisy drum or will not tumble

Rattles can be a zip in the drum — empty it and look. A grinding drum with no clothes in it is bearings or rollers, which is a repair. Do not run a dryer that smells of hot rubber belt until it is checked.

Safe checks you can make

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in Stop and get help applies.

Lint / fluff filter every load

Lift the filter from the door opening or rim, peel the fluff off, and slot it back fully. A half-seated filter can blow lint into the machine.

Condenser and water tank

On condenser dryers the tank pulls out at the top; the condenser slides out behind a lower front flap. Rinse fluff off the condenser until the water runs clear. Switch off and let it cool first.

Vents and the room

Vented dryers need an unobstructed hose to outside — not a crushed pipe behind the machine. All dryers need space around the back. A bone-dry cupboard with no air is a poor place to dry laundry.

Stop and get help

These are the points where another cycle, another click, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

Scorching, smoke or a lint fire

Unplug if you can do it safely, get people out if there is smoke, and do not open a roaring dryer to “see”. Lint fires are real.

Breaker trips when it heats

Leave it isolated. That is not a filter problem.

Cabinet too hot to touch

Stop using it. Overheat cut-outs exist because dryers start fires when airflow is gone.

Know your appliance

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, in plain language. This is identification, not a strip-down guide.

Heater / heat pump

Older vented and condenser models use a heater bank. Heat-pump models use a refrigeration circuit — not something to “top up” at home.

Thermostats and cut-outs

These open when the air gets too hot. Replacing them without fixing the blockage that caused the trip repeats the fault.

Moisture sensor

Metal strips in the drum that detect damp clothes. They fail, but filthy bars also lie — wipe them with a bit of white vinegar on a cloth, machine off.

Find your model number

Look around the door opening, just inside, or on a rear/side plate. Note whether it is vented, condenser or heat pump if the sticker says so — drying advice changes.

  • Photograph the model before you pull the machine out; rear plates are easy to miss in poor light.
  • Vented dryers have a hose or outlet at the back; condenser and heat-pump models have a pull-out water tank near the top — knowing which you have changes the advice.

When you start a diagnosis, use the camera button on the composer to photograph the plate. Conversations stay in your browser — they are not published as web pages.
