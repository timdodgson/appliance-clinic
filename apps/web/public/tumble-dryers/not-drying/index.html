Tumble dryer not drying

Almost every “not drying” complaint starts with airflow, not a dead heater. First work out which type you have: a vented dryer pushes warm, moist air out through a hose; a condenser dryer turns it into water in a tank; a heat-pump dryer also uses a tank but runs cooler and slower on purpose. Clear the filter and airflow before assuming the heater or thermostat has failed — a blocked machine is the usual culprit.

Common causes

A short, ordered list of what usually explains this — most likely and most accessible first. We cannot know your exact fault from here, only point you at the right area.

Blocked lint / fluff filter

The number-one cause on every type. A furred filter chokes the airflow so clothes tumble in air that cannot carry moisture away.

Restricted airflow

On vented models a crushed or blocked hose, or no outlet to outside, traps the damp air. All dryers need breathing space around the back.

Condenser blocked or water tank full

Condenser and heat-pump models stop drying when the condenser is furred with fluff or the water tank is full and not emptied.

Overloading or a very wet load

A drum crammed full, or washing that was not spun properly first, simply cannot dry in a normal cycle.

Heat-pump drying cool by design

Heat-pump dryers feel barely warm — that is normal. They dry gently and slowly, and a neglected filter makes them run even cooler, take forever, or throw an error.

No-heat or sensor fault

After airflow is cleared: a failed element, thermostat or a tripped safety cut-out gives a drum that turns but stays cold. A dirty moisture sensor can also end the cycle early thinking the load is dry.

Safe checks you can do

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in When to stop applies.

Clean the lint filter every load

Lift the filter from the door or rim, peel the fluff off, and slot it fully back. A half-seated filter blows lint deeper into the machine.

Condenser and water tank (condenser / heat-pump)

Switch off and let it cool. Empty the water tank, then rinse the pull-out condenser until the water runs clear. A blocked condenser mimics “the heater is dead”.

Vented hose and room

On a vented dryer, check the hose to outside is not crushed or clogged, and that the room is not sealed. A bone-dry cupboard with no air is a poor place to dry laundry.

Load size and spin

Dry a smaller load, and make sure it was spun in the washer first. Clothes dripping into the drum will never dry in a standard cycle.

When to stop

These are the points where another cycle, another go, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

Scorching, smoke or a burning smell

Unplug if you can do it safely, get people away if there is smoke, and do not open a roaring dryer to look. Lint plus heat is how laundry fires start.

Isolate before cleaning

Switch off at the wall before clearing the filter, condenser or vent. Let a hot machine cool first.

Cabinet too hot or trips on heat

If it overheats, trips the supply when it heats, or the case is too hot to touch, stop using it. Do not bypass thermal cut-outs, and do not test heaters live.

What part might be involved

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, framed as likely possibilities — not a certainty, and not a shopping list to buy before the fault is confirmed.

Heater or heat pump

Vented and older condenser models use a heater bank; heat-pump models use a sealed refrigeration circuit that is never a “top-up” job at home.

Thermostats and cut-outs

These trip when the air gets too hot. Fitting a new one without clearing the blockage that tripped it just repeats the fault.

Moisture sensor

Metal bars in the drum that sense damp clothes. Grimy bars can read wrong and end a cycle early.

Find your model number

Look around the door opening, just inside, or on a rear or side plate. Note whether the plate says vented, condenser or heat pump — the drying advice changes with the type.

  • Vented dryers have a hose or outlet at the back; condenser and heat-pump models have a pull-out water tank near the top.
  • Photograph the model before you pull the machine out; rear plates are easy to miss in poor light.

When you start a diagnosis, use the camera button on the composer to photograph the plate. Conversations stay in your browser — they are not published as web pages.
