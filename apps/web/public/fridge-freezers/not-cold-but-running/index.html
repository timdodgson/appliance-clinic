Fridge-freezer running but not cold

If the motor hums but the inside is not cold, think airflow and frost before the sealed system. A lot of “fridge warm, freezer still okay” faults are a blocked internal vent or an iced-up evaporator fan starving the fridge of cold air. A compressor that is silent or just clicks is a different story. One rule holds throughout: refrigerant and sealed-system work is never a DIY job.

Common causes

A short, ordered list of what usually explains this — most likely and most accessible first. We cannot know your exact fault from here, only point you at the right area.

Blocked vents or an overpacked cabinet

Cold air is shared between compartments through small vents. Pack the shelves tight against them and one area warms up while the other seems fine.

Iced-up evaporator fan

On frost-free models a fan blows cold air from behind a rear panel. If it ices up, the freezer can still feel cold while the fridge slowly warms.

Worn door seal or door left ajar

A tired or torn seal, or a door that does not quite shut, lets warm, moist air in — poor cooling and extra frost.

Settings or a recent warm load

A holiday setting, a knocked thermostat, or a big warm shop just put in can all look like a cooling fault for a while.

Dusty rear coils or no ventilation gap

Coils caked in dust, or a cabinet jammed into a tight recess, make the system run constantly and still cool poorly.

Compressor or sealed-system fault

A compressor that is silent, clicks and gives up, or runs forever while staying warm, points at the sealed system. That is an engineer’s job, not a DIY fix.

Safe checks you can do

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in When to stop applies.

Check the setting and recent loading

Make sure it is not on a holiday or off setting, nothing knocked the dial, and you have not just loaded a lot of warm food. Give it a few hours to recover after a big shop.

Door seal test

Close the door on a sheet of paper — it should drag as you pull it out. Gaps or tears let warm air in. Check the door is not being held ajar by a drawer or bottle.

Keep vents clear

Do not pack food against the internal vents or the rear wall. Cold air needs a path around the cabinet.

Clean the rear coils

Unplug, ease the cabinet out carefully with help, and vacuum dust from the rear coils and the floor beneath. Leave the ventilation gap the manufacturer specifies — usually a few centimetres.

When to stop

These are the points where another cycle, another go, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

No refrigerant DIY

Do not puncture pipework or try to “regas” the system. Cans sold online are not a fix, and damaging the sealed circuit writes the appliance off. This is regulated, engineer-only work.

Electrical burning or trips

Unplug it. A fridge that trips the supply can be the compressor or defrost heater and should be left isolated.

Food safety — sensible, not alarmist

If the fridge has been warm for hours, judge the food calmly: keep the door shut to buy time, and when something perishable has been in the danger zone too long, when in doubt, throw it out. Diagnosis will not make yesterday’s chicken safe.

What part might be involved

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, framed as likely possibilities — not a certainty, and not a shopping list to buy before the fault is confirmed.

Evaporator fan

Moves cold air around frost-free models. If it is iced or stalled, the fridge warms while the freezer may still feel cold.

Defrost heater and drain

Clear frost from the evaporator on auto-defrost models. When they fail, ice builds on the back wall — do not chip it off.

Door seals

A cheap-feeling part with a big effect. Worth describing before anyone mentions a compressor.

Compressor and sealed system

The heart of the cooling circuit. Named only as a possibility — any work here is for a qualified engineer.

Find your model number

Look inside the fridge on a side wall, beside the salad drawers, or behind a kick plate; some plates are on the rear. You want the model, not the litre capacity on the sales badge.

  • Take the photo with the door open in good light — condensation hides the digits.
  • On an American-style double door, check both liners for the plate.

When you start a diagnosis, use the camera button on the composer to photograph the plate. Conversations stay in your browser — they are not published as web pages.
