Hob help

A hob is only the cooking surface: gas burners, ceramic radiant zones, or induction coils under glass. It is not the oven underneath (if you have a separate cooker, use the oven page for the cavity). The useful question is which zone failed, whether it clicks, and whether the glass is intact.

Common problems

Short, honest explanations — not a parts catalogue. If it matches what you are seeing, you can take that wording into a diagnosis.

One zone dead, others fine

That pattern usually belongs to one burner, one radiant element, or one induction coil/inverter — not the whole mains. Say which zone (front left, etc.) and whether it is gas, ceramic or induction.

Diagnose this

Whole hob dead — no zones, no display

When nothing lights and no zone heats, think supply before board. Many hobs sit on their own fused spur or a cooker switch that is easy to knock off; a tripped RCD or a blown fuse in the spur kills the lot. After a power cut, some hobs will not respond until the clock is set or a child lock is cleared. A hob that stays completely dead with a confirmed live supply is a control-board or internal fault — not something you reset from the top of the glass.

Diagnose this

A zone cuts in and out while cooking

Induction zones share power and protect themselves from heat: a big pan on boost can make a neighbouring zone drop power, and a zone sat over a hot inverter will throttle or pause until it cools. That is designed behaviour, not a fault. A zone that flickers on any pan, buzzes in a new way, or cuts with a repeated gap can instead be a loose connection or a tiring coil — note whether it is one zone only or the whole hob.

Diagnose this

Induction will not detect a pan

The pan must be ferrous and roughly the size of the zone. A small pan on a large zone, or aluminium/copper without a steel base, looks like a broken hob. Try a magnet on the pan base. If a known-good pan fails on every zone, that is the hob.

Diagnose this

Gas clicks but will not light

Ignition can click while the gas valve is closed, a jet is blocked with overboil, or the burner cap is sat crooked. If you smell gas while it is clicking, stop — that is not a “keep clicking” situation.

Diagnose this

Error or fault code on the display

Induction and ceramic hobs flash codes like F, E or a symbol for overheating, a pan problem, or a sensor fault. The same letter means different things on different brands, so photograph the display and note what you were doing — boost on one zone, an empty pan left on, or a spill across the controls. An “L” or padlock symbol is a child lock, not a breakdown.

Diagnose this

Cracked ceramic or induction glass

A cracked hob top is a stop-using-it fault. Liquids can reach live parts. Do not “make do” with foil over the crack.

Diagnose this

Touch controls dead or locked

A child lock, a wet control panel, or a power cut that left the hob in lock can look like a total failure. Dry the glass and look for an ‘L’ or key symbol before assuming a control board.

Safe checks you can make

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in Stop and get help applies.

Cookware (induction)

Magnet sticks to the base: probably suitable. Very warped bases sit badly. This check avoids a false “hob is dead” on a new pan.

Burner caps sat flat (gas)

When the hob is cold, lift the pan support and check the cap is centred. Overboil debris in the spark ignitor can be brushed off gently — machine off, no solvents in the jet.

Isolation switch

Many hobs have a fused spur nearby. Confirm it is on. Do not open the spur.

Clear a lock or a power-cut sulk

If the controls look dead, dry the glass fully, then hold the lock or key button for a few seconds to clear a child lock. After a power cut some hobs need the time set before any zone will fire. These cost nothing and rule out a false “dead board”.

Stop and get help

These are the points where another cycle, another click, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

Gas smell

Same as cookers: no switches, ventilate, out if strong, 0800 111 999. Do not keep sparking a burner that is flooding gas.

Cracked glass

Switch off at the isolation point. Do not cook on a cracked ceramic or induction surface.

Burning under the glass or trips

Isolate. Residual heat warnings can stay on after a fault — still treat trips as electrical, not as a pan problem.

Know your appliance

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, in plain language. This is identification, not a strip-down guide.

Radiant element / induction coil

Under the glass. Identification diagrams are not a licence to lift a glued ceramic top. That work is a repair.

Ignition generator (gas)

Produces the click. If every burner is silent, the generator or its supply is a suspect. If only one is silent, look at that ignitor first.

Touch control / child lock

A software lock is not a failed board. Mention any key or padlock symbol on the display.

Find your model number

Often on the underside of the hob (you would only see it when the hob is out) or on the original paperwork. Some have a line on the rim under a pan stand. If you cannot see it without lifting a built-in hob, do not lift it — describe the brand from the glass and photograph any visible strip.

  • Never lever a sealed hob out to read a sticker; that is how you crack the worktop or the glass.
  • A photo of the control panel plus brand is still useful if the plate is inaccessible.

When you start a diagnosis, use the camera button on the composer to photograph the plate. Conversations stay in your browser — they are not published as web pages.
