Fan oven runs but doesn’t heat up

On a fan oven, the heating element is a hidden ring behind the back panel, wrapped around the fan. The fan can spin perfectly while that element is dead, so you get plenty of moving air and no real heat. Crucially, a working grill or a working second (top) oven runs on a different element and often a different circuit — so “the grill’s fine” tells you nothing about whether the fan-oven heating path is healthy.

Common causes

A short, ordered list of what usually explains this — most likely and most accessible first. We cannot know your exact fault from here, only point you at the right area.

Fan oven element failed

The hidden circular element behind the back panel is the classic cause of air-but-no-heat. It can fail without any visible damage.

Thermostat or temperature sensor

If the oven’s sensor misreads and thinks the cavity is already hot, the control will not call for heat. The fan still runs.

Clock or timer not set

Very common after a power cut: many ovens refuse to heat until the clock is set and any auto-timer is cleared. That is not an element failure.

Control board or selector

A selector switch or control that will not energise the fan-oven element can leave the fan running on its own. A possibility to name, not to assume.

Door not sealing

A dropped door or flattened seal leaks heat and gives pale, slow results — usually “not hot enough” rather than “no heat at all”.

Safe checks you can do

Switch the appliance off at the wall first unless the check is only looking at a display. Stop if anything in When to stop applies.

Set the clock and clear auto modes

After any power cut, set the time and make sure no automatic timer or “A” symbol is holding the oven off. This fixes a surprising number of “dead” ovens for free.

Confirm the oven has power

Built-in ovens are often on a switched fused spur above a cupboard, or a cooker switch on the wall. Confirm it is on before assuming an element has failed.

Note what is and is not working

Does the grill heat? The top oven? The light and fan? The pattern narrows things — but remember a working grill does not prove the fan-oven element is good, because they are separate.

Do not open the back or terminal block

Identifying the fault is as far as this goes. The element lives behind the rear panel; replacing it on a hard-wired cooker is an engineer job.

When to stop

These are the points where another cycle, another go, or a screwdriver makes things worse. ApplianceClinic is not a repair company and will not send an engineer to you.

Isolate before any inspection

Switch off at the cooker switch or consumer unit before looking inside the cavity. No live mains testing.

Sparks, burning or a tripped cooker circuit

Leave it isolated. Cooker circuits are high current and terminal-block burns at the back are an engineer repair, not a customer reseat.

Element replacement is not a first-step DIY

Swapping a fan element behind the back panel of a built-in, hard-wired cooker needs a competent engineer. Describe the symptoms rather than opening it up.

What part might be involved

Names you may hear in a diagnosis, framed as likely possibilities — not a certainty, and not a shopping list to buy before the fault is confirmed.

Fan oven element

The hidden ring around the fan. The most likely single cause of air-but-no-heat — but confirm it rather than assume it.

Thermostat / temperature sensor (NTC)

Tells the control how hot the cavity is. A faulty sensor can suppress heating even with a good element.

Control board or selector

Named as a possibility. These need an engineer and evidence; they are not a parts-first purchase.
